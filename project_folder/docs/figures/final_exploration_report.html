2. Pattern and Trend Visualization

The trend profile shows strong acceleration in recorded conflict events after the mid-2010s, while average GDP and governance indicators change more gradually.

Conflict burden is concentrated in a small set of countries with persistently high totals.

The causal diagram illustrates two mechanisms through which violence slows the structural shift out of agriculture: investment deterrence (infrastructure destruction, capital flight) and human capital depletion (deaths, displacement, school closures).

Governance split indicates higher median and upper-tail conflict in the low V-Dem group.

Principal Component Analysis

PCA on employment sectors, GDP, and governance reveals a dominant "development" dimension (PC1 explains 66.3% of variance). PC1+PC2 together explain 83.2% of variance.

Key Finding: The three employment sectors (agriculture, industry, services) are highly correlated with each other and with GDP, confirming they represent a single "development dimension." This suggests that in papers/presentations, these variables can be reduced to a composite index rather than analyzed separately.

4. Key Takeaways

  1. Conflict intensity is highly skewed: most country-years have low values, but a small set exhibits very high spikes.
  2. Simple bivariate relationships with GDP and labor shares are visible but weak in pooled linear correlation terms.
  3. Institutional quality (V-Dem rule index) shows a negative association with conflict and clearer distributional separation in boxplots.
  4. Missingness is structurally non-random (especially governance coverage), so interpretation should acknowledge data availability constraints.
  5. PCA confirms employment sectors, GDP, and governance load on a single "development" dimension, suggesting composite indices may be more powerful than individual variables.
